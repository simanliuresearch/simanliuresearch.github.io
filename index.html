About me

Communication researcher · image maker

Curious about the distance between what people see and what they believe.

I'm Siman Liu—a Ph.D. student in Communication at the University at Albany, SUNY.

Before I studied how people decide what to believe, I spent years trying to make things worth believing: editing multimedia stories in Hong Kong, framing portraits, cutting video until a moment felt true. Somewhere along the way I grew fascinated by the gap in between—the distance between the story a screen tells you and the story you're actually willing to trust.

That gap is now my research. I study media trust: how people judge whether the news is fair, how partisan identity and national loyalty quietly bend those judgments, and why two people watching the very same broadcast can walk away certain it was biased—in opposite directions. I work mostly with survey and quantitative methods, and I'm increasingly drawn to what AI-generated and synthetic media will do to trust that was already fragile.

I came to this from the visual side, and I've never quite left it. You'll find that half of me on the Through My Lens page.
