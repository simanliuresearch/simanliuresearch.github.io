Filtering the Feed

Out-Party Affect and Perceived Partisan Exposure on Facebook

Read the full proposal (PDF) Contact the author
Accepted · AEJMC 2026
  • Author Siman Liu · Department of Communication, University at Albany, SUNY
  • Project typeResearch in Progress
  • DataANES 2020 Social Media Study
  • Analytic sampleN = 3,123

Abstract

This study asks whether the partisan content people think they see on Facebook is shaped less by the feed itself than by how they already feel about the other side. Using panel data from the ANES 2020 Social Media Study (N = 3,123), I find that pre-existing out-party affect predicts how much partisan exposure people report—and that this perceived exposure is, in turn, associated with colder evaluations of opposing candidates after the election. The takeaway: partisan media environments may be partly perceptual and emotion-driven, not purely a matter of what the algorithm serves.

Preliminary Findings

  • More negative pre-election evaluations of the out-party candidate were associated with a greater likelihood of reporting exposure to out-party candidate content on Facebook.
  • Reported out-party exposure was associated with lower post-election out-party trait evaluations after accounting for baseline affect.
  • Reported out-party exposure conditioned the relationship between pre-election affect and post-election evaluations.
  • The in-party pathway was comparatively weaker: the corresponding interaction was not statistically significant.
  • Together, these patterns suggest that self-reported partisan exposure may partly reflect affect-conditioned attention, interpretation, or recall.

These findings are preliminary and should be interpreted as associations, not causal effects.
